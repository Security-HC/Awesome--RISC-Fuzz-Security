## 摘要

The company is in a new digital transformation era. The evolution of the organizations has progressed from transaction processing and enterprise integration to business intelligence, cloud computing, big data, machine learning, gen AI and now towards autonomous and agentic systems. However, technology is not intelligence, in an organization. This is no longer solely a matter of gathering more data, implementing more models and using the newest artificial intelligence tools. That's the challenge of how to create an enterprise-level capability that can help continuously convert observations into trusted information, information into insights and prediction, and knowledge into intelligence, and intelligence into governed actions leading to measurable results. This book tackles this issue from the engineering, architectural, operational and governance standpoint. The case for enterprise intelligence is defined as the socio-technical capability connecting a system of data, platforms, analytics, machine learning, generative AI, knowledge systems, autonomous agents, humans, processes, policies and feedback mechanisms. It is a technology-agnostic view that elevates decision quality, operational reliability and assured autonomy, learning, and business value to the heart of enterprise transformation. The book consists of a collection of Enterprise Intelligence Lifecycle, linking together data, information, insights, predictions, knowledge, intelligence, actions, and governed autonomy, all in an iterative process of continuous feedback. It's important to note that the lifecycle is not being presented as a straight technology pipeline. Each step raises semantic, ownership, reliability, security, governance, accountability and measurable value issues. The right idea is that intelligence is only an enterprise capability when technical products are linked to actual decisions, processes, stewards, process controls and outcomes. As such, the manuscript views intelligence as a continuously evolving product that is engineered to be an enterprise endeavor, and not anything specific like an isolated dashboard, machine learning model, chatbot, or AI agent. Chapter 1 lays the foundation for the concepts and architecture of the intelligent enterprise. It helps you define and distinguish between a data-driven organization and an intelligence-driven organization, while also introducing the Enterprise Intelligence Lifecycle, layered reference architecture, capability domains, maturity model, value measurement framework, principles of governance and an implementation blueprint. One key theme is keeping the goal based on appropriate and governed autonomy rather than the goal in front of it being maximum autonomy and automation. There must be a balance of autonomy to consequence, uncertainty, reversibility, level of evidence, permissions and escalation requirements. The second chapter focuses on Intelligence on the Platform. The way to build an Enterprise AI involves the ability to leverage a scalable, componentized and integrated set of projects, bespoke pipelines, manually deployed environments or approval queues. Instead the platform and the various components on it must be seen as an internal product, offering a coherent user journey, self-service, golden paths, cloud-native foundations, software supply-chain integrity, observability and reliability, security, cost management, and developer experience. The book focuses on re-usable capabilities and tangible benefits in engineering flow, reliability, risk, economics, and business results as three key differentiators between a platform and a portal, Kubernetes cluster, and centralized ops team. This Continuous Intelligence is built on a foundation of data engineering and a modern data platform, which is covered in the third and fourth chapters. In today's modern companies, it is essential to combine data warehouses, data lakes, data lake houses, data products, data mesh, data fabric, batch processing, streaming, change data capture, event-driven architectures, metadata, catalogs, data lineage, semantic models, data contracts, data quality, observability, security, privacy, resilience, and FinOps. The authors of the book expressly do not recommend any single architectural pattern as necessarily being the best. It promotes workload-driven architectures, however, based on latency, consistency, scale, governance, interoperability, reliability, team capabilities and costs. Also, Continuous Intelligence depends on reliable streams of data. As a result, batch, micro-batch, streaming, event time, stateful processing, delivery guarantees, idempotency, replay, schema evolution, orchestration, backfills, data contracts, testing, service-level objectives, security, privacy, lineage and recovery are discussed. The goal is not to move 100% of all workloads to become “real time” but to provide the right level of freshness, completeness, correctness, and confidence of data for the decision they are supporting. The fifth chapter takes a step from data engineering, to analytics engineering, to decision intelligence. There is a shift in understanding how to use Analytics – not merely as a reporting or visualization tool. It is the first large-scale point in time when the enterprise creates a common model of what is happening in the business. Grain, facts, dimensions, time, semantic models, metrics engineering, business ontologies, knowledge representation, data contracts, self-service analytics, analytical testing, lineage, experimentation, causal evidence, forecasting, scenario analysis, optimization and decision products these all come together to provide trustworthy analytical capabilities. The chapter highlights the difference between technically valid query, a reliable business measure and an operational decision system. Production decision capabilities are discussed in the sixth chapter as machine learning systems. Machine learning is more than an algorithm-selection challenge; it's a question of problem framing, decision value, training evidence, labels, temporal validation, leakage prevention, feature engineering, feature stores, experimentation, reproducibility, calibration, thresholds, models usefulness, training architecture, deployment, model serving, security, privacy, fairness, reliability, and cost. The pathways to a production accountable for progress based on an initially promising notebook is a key factor in the debate. Chapter Seven deals with the concept of MLOps and LLMOps, as production AI alters in a myriad ways other than just in application code. System behavior can vary based on data sources, labels, features, models, thresholds, prompts, retrieval indexes, context, foundation models, tools, memories and policies. Operations discipline via MLOps and LLMOps, thus, is essential for continuous integration, continuous delivery, continuous training, continuous evaluation, experiment tracking, registries, lineage, progressive delivery, monitoring, incident response, rollback, security, privacy, human oversight and cost control. Specifically, LLMOps considers the entire generative AI app (not just the foundation model) to be the production system. The eighth chapter looks at generative AI, enterprise knowledge systems, retrieval-augmented generation, foundation models, knowledge graphs, semantic layers, multimodal intelligent systems, and knowledge-intensive work. One key argument is that the enterprise knowledge cannot be captured as the pre-trained knowledge of a language model. The knowledge of the enterprise needs to be handled in a controlled manner, using governed sources of truth, Semantic structures, retrieval, reranking, context assembly, authorization, provenance, citation, freshness, evaluation, and uncertainty management. GenAI is thus proposed as a knowledge-interaction layer under human control and not an independent Oracle. The ninth chapter then takes this architecture to the next level to agentic AI and autonomous business systems. The novelties of agents, which can plan, invoke tools, maintain a state, interact with environments, and perform multi-step actions, make them inductive of a qualitatively different engineering problem. So, the chapter covers goal formulation, planning, execution, reflection, tool contracts, least-privilege permissions, state and memory considerations, identities, single-agent and multi-agent designs, orchestration, evaluation, security considerations, observability, recovery, human oversight and progressive autonomy. The guiding principle isn't "as free as possible," but rather “useful autonomy” which is bounded, observable, interruptible, recoverable and proportional to consequence. The final chapter makes these technical capabilities come together via governance and scaling of the intelligent enterprise. Architecture isn't enough for enterprise transformation. All of these topics strategy, portfolio management, value realization, risk-tiered governance, platform architecture, team topologies, product ownership, security, privacy, compliance, sovereignty, resilience, financial management, sustainable engineering, workforce design, adoption, outcome measurement, and federated scaling – are essential for success. Final chapters intertwine the entire Enterprise Intelligence Lifecycle into an operating model for the EI lifecycle, and outline additional validation questions and areas for further research. An underlying theme throughout the book is that governance does not occur as an ‘add-on' after deployment. Security, privacy, fairness, transparency, accountability, reliability, observability, cost management, auditability and human oversight are also "cross-cutting" architectural concerns. As a result, the manuscript brings into the picture enterprise architecture in relation to current AI approaches on AI risk management, AI governance, responsible AI, trustworthy AI, privacy engineering, software supply-chain security, model accountability, and regulatory compliance. The risk of value becomes greater as AI becomes more pervasive in an organization, which also means greater systemic and organizational risk. A second pivotal aspect of this book is the use of evidence and feedback. The real measure of enterprise intelligence should be in the forms of business outcomes, decision quality, adoption, reliability, risk, the velocity of learning, speed and cost. Technical correctness of the model, or the platform's usability or the quality of the AI application's response alone is not enough to be enterprise value. Whether the goal of the complete decision system enhances an important decision outcome within acceptable operational, ethical, financial and regulatory constraints. This view also emphasizes the importance of experimentation, causal inference, observability, incident learning, continuous evaluation in enterprise intelligence. The book is geared for various target groups. The material is designed to be used by enterprise architects, data architects, cloud architects, platform engineers, data engineers, analytics engineers, machine learning engineers, MLOps and LLMOps practitioners, in addition to AI engineers, software engineers, technology leaders, CIOs, CTOs, CDOs, Chief AI Officers, product managers, risk professionals, security leaders, governance professionals, researchers, educators, and graduate students, based on their specific responsibilities. The chapters are carefully designed to be both accessible for conceptual learning and easy to put into practice: learning objectives, architecture, design principles, enterprise scenarios, decision tables, maturity models, implementation roadmaps, chapter summaries, key take-aways, discussion questions and references. The book also aims to inform the readers about the several communities talked about separately: Data Management, Data Engineering, Software Engineering, Cloud Computing, Platform Engineering, Analytics, Machine Learning, Artificial Intelligence, Generative AI, Knowledge Management, Autonomous Systems, Cybersecurity, Privacy, Enterprise Architecture, DevOps, SRE, FinOps, AI governance, and organizational transformation. Any one of these disciplines is insufficient to build the intelligent enterprise. Its performance is dependent on what interfaces among them are. We've introduced new frameworks that provide a common context in which to think about these interfaces, such as the Enterprise Intelligence Lifecycle, the Layered Enterprise Intelligence Reference Architecture, the Semantic Reliability and Decision-Product Framework, the Progressive Autonomy and Governed Learning Model, the Data-to-Decision Reliability Chain, the AI Change Evidence Chain, the Knowledge Trust Chain, and the Cohesive Enterprise Intelligence Operating Model. It is not meant to recommend nor mandate any specific vendor, any particular set of technologies, any particular cloud provider, any particular family of models, or any particular organization. Instead, they offer reusable engineering abstractions that enterprises can use to consider alternatives and fix a line of accountability from experimentation to production and back again to continuous learning. This book therefore has a fairly straightforward, albeit all-important, main theme: An intelligent enterprise is not bought, it is engineered. Cloud services, data platforms, machine learning models, foundation models, retrieval systems, copilots, and autonomous agents are all critical components, yet each serves as a foundation to achieve enterprise intelligence on its own. When combined with reliable data, common application of meaning, powerful platforms, production engineering, responsible decision-making mechanisms, metrics, governance and human decision, and feedback, this is where intelligence comes into play. The strategic question will not only be “how much can I deploy?” anymore as organisations advance toward the one becoming more capable with artificial intelligence. It will be their ability to sense, comprehend, forecast, recall, rationalize, decide, do, manage and gauge, restore and learn effectively. As businesses evolve these technologies into reliable, secure, accountable and economically sustainable reusable systems, they will be better equipped to scale and benefit from innovation while maintaining reliability, security, accountability and sustainability. This book is presented as a how-to, how-why text on that trip. It's also an invitation to see how data and AI are not just tools, but also a unified approach to enterprise engineering, where architecture, platforms, data, analytics, machine learning, generative AI, agentic systems, data governance, people and organizational learning, and experiences are in closer collaboration to build and deliver measurable and sustainable AI.
